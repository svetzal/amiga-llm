One unattended run of the expressive piano floppy, from the boot to the end of its tenth piece. Nobody touches the machine. Fifteen seconds after its screen appears it plays the first piece, and fifteen seconds after each piece ends it plays the next.

The machine is a stock NTSC Amiga 500 with 1 MB of memory and Kickstart 1.2, emulated cycle-exact in Amiberry 8.5.0. The pieces are human performances, with each note's velocity and the sustain pedal. A 68000 program plays them through Paula, the Amiga's sound chip: four 8-bit voices, from samples of a real grand piano. The samples, the scores and the program were made with modern tools on a Mac; the Amiga does the playing. The line of the piece playing turns gold, and the scopes at the bottom show the four voices and their mix.

This is the emulator's own picture and sound, every frame and every sample, with nothing cut: the boot, the gaps between pieces, and every piece in full. Each piece starts about a second after its line turns gold, because each recording starts with that much silence.

The project: https://github.com/svetzal/amiga-llm (EXP-004, the expressive piano)
